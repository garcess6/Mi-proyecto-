Seventy million people in the Democratic Republic of the Congo don't have even basic drinking water. That's more people than live in France.
Almost nine in ten Congolese don't have safe water at home, and eight million drink straight from rivers, lakes and streams.
Unsafe water spreads cholera, typhoid and diarrhoea. In 2025, cholera hit sixteen of the country's twenty-six provinces: nearly forty-seven thousand cases, and over thirteen hundred deaths, in just seven months.
The government, WHO and partners are responding: vaccination campaigns for millions, treatment centres, and water treatment tablets for families. African leaders have set a goal: end cholera outbreaks by 2030.
What can families do today? Boiling works, but needs fuel. Filters need upkeep. Chlorine struggles in cloudy river water.
AquaCore's chlorine dioxide tablets: one tablet treats twenty-five litres in thirty minutes, works in cloudy water, costs about fifty cents per person per month, and was tested by national labs in Africa.
