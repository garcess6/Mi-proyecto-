Boiling, filters, chlorine, tablets. Which one makes water safe? Let's see what each one really does.
Boiling: a rolling boil for at least one minute kills germs. But it doesn't remove chemicals like arsenic or nitrates, and it needs fuel.
Filters trap dirt and many germs. But it depends on the filter: many don't stop viruses or chemicals, and they need cleaning.
Chlorine kills most bacteria and viruses, and keeps protecting stored water. But it needs time to work, works worse in cloudy water, and some parasites resist it.
Tablets are easy to dose, carry and store: no fuel, no equipment. That makes them practical for reaching large populations. Some, like chlorine dioxide tablets, also work in cloudy water and against tough parasites. Just look for lab-tested results.
So no single method removes everything. Test first, choose the right treatment, combine methods if needed, and store water safely.
